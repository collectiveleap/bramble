Low-fidelity sketches · Bramble experiments 1 and 2 · Composer Sandbox data

Eight ways to show what the experiments produced

Each sketch uses the real data from experiment 2: one invented email from Ana Ruiz, three quotes, three Bramble statements, and five Inquiry-frame bindings. These are layouts to react to, not working tools. Where a sketch shows something Bramble does not store today, it says so.

5 · Stance: show the reasoning

Notes at the foot of the page

Borrowed from scholarly editions, which print a clean reading text and put every source decision in numbered notes below it.
Seeker: Ana Ruiz, the founder.1
Respondent: the advisor she is writing to.2
Stakes: about seven months of runway,3 weighed against dilution and pricing power4 if she raises before any revenue.5
Presuppositions: that a seed round is available now; that waiting brings a conversion; that the timing is hers.6
Gaps: whether investors expect revenue; whether a pilot converts in time; what dilution costs now against later.7
1Grounded in the question statement · cognition-derived · Claude · “her name comes from the source's sender.”
2No grounding. Nothing in the email names who she is writing to.
3Grounded in the runway statement · cognition-derived · Claude.
4Inferred; not grounded in any statement (from the link's note).
5Grounded in the pilots statement · cognition-derived · Claude.
6Grounded in the question statement · “taken for granted by the way the question is framed.”
7Grounded in all three statements · “what would have to be known to answer the question as asked.”

What it shows well: the result reads cleanly and every claim has a findable note. Limit today: notes 3, 4 and 5 split one value into phrases. Bramble stores grounding per binding, not per phrase, so I made that split by hand from the link's note.
