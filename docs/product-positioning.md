# Product Positioning

Bramble's stance toward the market: what the tool is for, how it differs from the dominant personal-knowledge-management (PKM) tools, and where its center of gravity sits. The design principles that follow from this stance live in the sibling [principles reference](principles.md).

## The tool's job

The tool's job is to preserve the structure produced by applying blends as a byproduct of natural communication — not to "help you think." The cognitive work happens elsewhere: in conversation, in interview rooms, on walks, in the head. The tool records the result faithfully and structures it, with all its layered relationships intact.

## Inverting the PKM stance

This inverts the dominant PKM stance. Tools like Tana, Roam, Obsidian, and Notion assume the tool is *where* thinking happens. Here the value proposition is different: the tool preserves the layering of thinking over time, so that one's collaborators can reason about the reasoning later. It is a methodology workbench — a place where the layered history of how a group has *been* applying blends accumulates incidentally, made structurally rich by AI in the background, and surfaceable on demand when anyone needs to see what is underneath.

## Center of gravity

This positions the tool outside Tana, Notion, Plectica, Slack, and Loom. Its center of gravity is not helping people think in the moment; it is preserving the structure of how groups have been thinking, so later thinking can build on it rather than recapitulate it.
